About me

Performance thinking, applied to games and simulation.

I'm studying Computer Science in Real-Time Interactive Simulation at DigiPen Institute of Technology Singapore (SIT-DigiPen), after a diploma in Computer Engineering at Singapore Polytechnic. I specialise in graphics programming, real-time simulation and networking. My favourite problems are the ones where the clock is tight: a collision system that has to finish inside a frame, a pathfinder that has to answer before the player notices.

I like to measure first, then optimise: benchmarking different bounding volumes, swapping a list for a binary min-heap, initialising nodes lazily. I'm now learning how that story continues below the application, with x86-64 assembly and the System V ABI, and I'd like to move towards performance testing and benchmarking at the application and infrastructure level.

I also serve as President of the DigiPen Student Management Committee, and I used to command a platoon as an infantry officer in the Singapore Armed Forces. Both taught me to communicate clearly and follow issues through to the end.

Projects

Featured · DigiPen full-stack project

PinPoint (BowlEye)

An Android app that coaches bowling form using on-device pose detection, backed by its own account and video server, a project website and a self-hosted CI/CD pipeline.

  • Pose detection: CameraX frames go through MediaPipe's pose landmarker on the GPU at roughly 15 to 23 fps, driving a live skeleton overlay and step and phase detection.
  • Native menu: the main menu is a C++ OpenGL ES game shell talking to Kotlin through a platform bridge.
  • Backend: a Go API with SQLite, invite-only sign-up, hashed passwords, video uploads with limits and admin tools.
  • Infrastructure: Jenkins builds, tests and deploys after each merge, with health checks and automatic rollback, continuous database backups and Telegram alerts.

My role: Live Feedback Manager, responsible for the live feedback side of the app.

Physics, collision and benchmarking

Stretch

Stretch is a grapple platformer. As Product Manager I coordinated scope, timeline and deliverables for an 8-person team over a 7-month agile cycle. As sole owner of physics and math, I wrote AABB collision, slope-based movement, rigid-body response and a grapple traversal mechanic (attach, extend, spring-back) from scratch.

I also built its spatial data structure and collision detection system in C++, and benchmarked bounding-volume approaches (Ritter's and PCA-based spheres) alongside ray casting for triangle intersection to compare their performance.

Game AI and pathfinding

N Fighters

N Fighters has an adaptive AI opponent. I led an N-gram model in C++ that reads the player's input history and predicts their next counter-move, so the opponent adapts to real-time strategy. I also built the core game loop and a coroutine-driven state machine.

In my pathfinding coursework I implemented A* with five heuristics, Floyd-Warshall and Goal Bounding, then cut computation time with a binary min-heap open list and lazy node initialisation. There's a live version of the idea in the playground below.
